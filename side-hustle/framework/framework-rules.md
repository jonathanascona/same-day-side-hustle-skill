# Framework Rules

## Feasible

The same day version must fit the user's real time, skills, budget, tools, permissions, and access. It should produce buyer evidence within a week.

## Profitable

The offer needs a plausible price, positive contribution margin, and a believable path to a first transaction. Revenue scenarios are not promises.

## Persuasive

A buyer must quickly understand the outcome and why this option is preferable. The offer should be repeatable in two sentences.

## Promise

Lead with the buyer's result, not the mechanism.

## Pitch

Explain who it is for, what is delivered, and how it creates the result.

## Price

State the unit, inclusions, risk reduction, and next action. A free offer needs a measurable conversion purpose.

## Presence

Create one link or artifact that explains the offer.

## Acquisition

Choose one channel containing reachable buyers.

## Fulfillment

Define a workflow the user can execute immediately.

## Conversion

Use a payment, deposit, booking, preorder, application, or qualified response.

## Compression rule

When the original idea is too large:

1. preserve the outcome
2. replace software with a manual workflow
3. narrow the buyer
4. narrow the use case
5. reduce frequency or volume
6. sell a pilot
7. remove infrastructure not required for the first transaction

## Evidence ladder

From weakest to strongest:

1. personal excitement
2. search interest or broad market data
3. competitor existence
4. problem interviews
5. qualified replies
6. booked calls or applications
7. deposits or preorders
8. payments
9. repeat usage or repeat purchase
10. referrals and retention

Describe evidence at its actual level.
