# Framework

## Stage 1: Understand the idea

The workflow identifies:

- buyer
- problem
- desired outcome
- mechanism
- deliverable
- acquisition channel
- founder advantage
- constraints

Unknowns are labeled rather than silently treated as facts.

## Stage 2: Research only what changes the decision

When research tools are available, the skill looks for:

- direct and adjacent alternatives
- customer language
- pricing anchors
- reachable buyer channels
- legal, platform, privacy, data, or technical blockers

Research is time boxed. The goal is not a market report. The goal is a better launch decision.

## Stage 3: Score the opportunity

The score is based on three categories:

### Feasible

Can the proposed version be created, fulfilled, and exposed to buyers with the user's actual time, budget, permissions, and skills?

### Profitable

Is there a believable price, positive unit economics, and a realistic path to a first transaction?

### Persuasive

Can a buyer quickly understand the result, the deliverable, and the reason to choose this offer?

The score is paired with hard gates and an evidence confidence grade. A high score cannot override a failed legal, buyer, economics, fulfillment, capital, time, or dependency gate.

## Stage 4: Compress the idea

The workflow separates:

- long term vision
- today's sellable slice
- validation artifact
- non goals

It prefers manual or productized delivery when that creates trustworthy evidence faster than software.

## Stage 5: Create the offer

The offer contains:

- promise
- pitch
- price
- unit of sale
- inclusions
- variable cost
- risk reduction
- call to action
- sales page copy
- objection handling

## Stage 6: Build the launch system

Every launch needs four functions:

### Presence

One shareable place that explains the offer.

### Acquisition

One channel containing reachable buyers.

### Fulfillment

A workflow that can deliver what was promised.

### Conversion

A payment, deposit, booking, preorder, application, or qualified response.

## Stage 7: Model the economics

The workflow calculates:

- price
- fees
- variable cost
- gross profit
- fulfillment time
- effective hourly gross profit
- break even sales
- scenario revenue

The scenarios are labeled as assumptions, not forecasts.

## Stage 8: Prepare or build

Plan mode keeps the output in chat. Prepare mode creates a launch packet. Build mode also implements and verifies the smallest credible vertical slice.

## Stage 9: Handoff

The final handoff distinguishes:

- complete
- prepared but unverified
- blocked
- intentionally deferred

It ends with one concrete next action and 48 hour thresholds for continuing, revising, or stopping.
